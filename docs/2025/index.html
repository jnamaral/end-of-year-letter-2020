2025: The Beginning of a Long Goodbye

This end-of-year letter, also available in Portuguese. Previous editions are found here.

Please reply to jamaral@ualberta.ca.

She climbed to the basecamp of Mount Everest, twice. Climbing to the peak was never the plan. Her focus has always been the trail and the walking, discovering new paths, the mind-clearing process of a long trail ahead. In the Caucasus in Russia, some of the hike was through deep snow. The yak-milk-based diet in Mongolia was a challenge for someone who never liked any milk products. A tour of the interior of Iran revealed surprising dry landscapes and beautiful mosques. In South Africa she saw the animals, but was more interested in the walking. The paths in the South of Chile were cold but the views were magnificent. The coast of the island of Santa Catarina was just another cyclic path to be walked for many days. In her travels, she has never been one to linger in a museum, or to make detailed reports of her travels, or even to talk much about what she saw at a party. She always enjoyed the independence of her connection with her fellow walkers, her true social circle. When the economy turned and led to unfavorable exchange rates for Brazilians to travel abroad, her long trails were in Brazil. Sometimes they were religious paths. She did not care for the religious aspects, but she wanted the walking with her fellow walkers.

When her memory started failing, first attributed to COVID in 2020, and when she was no longer able to book, pay, or manage the administration of her travels, a dear friend started doing all that for her. The friends started looking after her when needed, but she continued with the travels, trails, and walks.

She never had a partner or kids, always leading an independent life. When she was young, she had to wait for her turn to go to university because she had to work to support herself from a young age. But eventually she completed a degree in Electrical Engineering, only to find out that for a female graduating close to the age of 30, there were no engineering jobs. She soldiered on and was selected for a position in the Superior Labour Court for her state. With the new position, she went back to school to study Law. She never told anyone that she was back in school — at night while working full time — until she was close to graduation. She finished Law School and advanced in her position at work to serve as one of the financial auditors for the Court. The financial independence of this job enabled her to start her trekking around the world. She retired early because she felt that she was no longer as sharp as she once was.

In December 2024, during a week-long family gathering at the beach in Xangri-lá, it became clear that her cognitive deficiencies were more than the memory lapses that she attributed to long COVID. An MRI in April indicated significant reductions in brain volumes pointing to Alzheimer’s, but a more precise MRI in July confirmed that it is a non-Alzheimer neuropathology. Psycho-neural tests led to a probable diagnosis: a Semantic Variation of Fronto-Temporal Dementia (FTD).

Since January, I have been working with my Mom, my brother, and Salete’s closest walking friends to help diagnose what is going on with Salete and to plan for the support that she will need in the coming years. FTD is a progressive neuro-degenerative disease. There is no treatment. The Salete that we knew is no longer there. She is still living independently, but requires support for all her financial and administrative activities. She is no longer able to make appointments or attend health-care appointments. Even simple shopping activities can be challenging. Thus, my Mom, at the age of 86, is becoming her caregiver. Both my brother Marcos and I are far away. We try to help with the management and to provide the support that we can. I am visiting Porto Alegre whenever I can. I was there in January, May, October, and am back to spend the Christmas–New Year time with them. FTD has left her with neurodiversities that are similar to the ones we observe in our son Daniel. Thus, my experience with Daniel has helped with understanding and planning around her new neurodiversities.

When we progressively learned about the diagnosis, my Mom had the best reaction: "It is what it is and we will do the best that we can." I have witnessed my Mom becoming much more independent, decisive, and active this year. At some point she said: "I discovered that my mission is not done yet and I do not have time to be old." This attitude, and her unshakable role as the matriarch who protects, guides, consoles, and supports, does not come as a surprise.

During the process of trying to get Salete to seek medical help and navigating the diagnostic tests, we discovered that she has truly committed friends who were willing to do so much to support her. We call them her guardian angels. Among them, Mercedes and Natali stand out. It was not surprising to discover the depth of the commitment of my Mom’s circle of friends, nephews, grandchildren to support my Mom as she, at the age of 86, starts taking care of my sister. We are very thankful for all who are supporting us as we walk this path with her. My circle of friends has also been very supportive and patient listeners as I work through processing this new reality and what I need to do to support my Mom and my sister.

At the university, I moved my heavy teaching load to the Winter term, which kept me grounded in Edmonton from January to April, with the exception of a week in Las Vegas to attend a conference in March — my last trip to the USA for a long while — and a work trip to Toronto in February to connect with my industrial collaborators there.

During the Winter months, Scott was busy as a volunteer instructor for basic avalanche training for backcountry skiing. He was the volunteer organizer, with Alpine Canada, for a training course. They delivered in-class training in Edmonton over several weeks and then took small groups to the mountains for their first experience in the backcountry. He was gone for several weekends to do this. He was happy to connect with many people who are also interested in backcountry skiing.

I onboarded several undergraduate students to my research lab in May. Before I started travelling again, we had time for Junior to invite all the students in the lab for our traditional Junior's walk to the trails next to the North Saskatechewan River.

Returning to Toronto in May to attend an international conference on performance engineering, a long-term professional connection, I met many colleagues from that area of research whom I had not seen in a while, and had the opportunity to have my first boat tour on a Canadian big lake, with fantastic views of Toronto.

My May-June trip to Brazil started with very intense days in Porto Alegre where I was accompanying Salete and my Mom to her first neurologist appointment. My brother Marcos came with his partner Nara, and we were also sorting out powers of attorney, banking accounts, and all the details needed for us to take over the administration of someone’s life.

After these four intense days in Porto Alegre, I went to Natal, a coastal capital in the Northeast of Brazil, where I spent two weeks delivering my workshop on effective communication in science. I visited beautiful beach spots, experienced the local music and culinary traditions, and had a good experience at the university.

My next stop on that trip was in my favourite city in Brazil: Belo Horizonte (BH). This visit was part of a research collaboration with my colleague Fernando Pereira, the most prominent and most productive compiler researcher in Latin America. Our joint research project the previous year was selected for funding with the highest scores in the competition. At Fernando’s insistence, I agreed to lead the communication workshop at the Federal University of Minas Gerais (UFMG) for the third time. This was sweet because this workshop was created for UFMG many years ago. It seems to be always popular there. I took advantage of a four-day holiday weekend in BH to explore the local live music scene. BH has a great nightlife with good music in surprising venues if you know what to look for. My M.Sc. student Danila Saliayeu spent three months in BH in an academic exchange. We went together to a Roda de Samba one evening.

I finished that visit to Brazil in São Paulo where I attended an international workshop on RISC-V, an open-source processor architecture that I use in my teaching. This workshop was organized by my friend and colleague Guido Araujo from Unicamp. It was great to meet several academic colleagues there.

While I was visiting Brazil in June, Scott organized a road trip for him and Daniel to Oklahoma — Daniel prefers not to fly if he can avoid it. Their road trip took them to Mount Rushmore, Yellowstone National Park, and Kansas City in Missouri where they went to a Royals baseball game. They went to a rodeo, visited Mesa Verde National Park, and went to Santa Fe in New Mexico. They also visited Scott’s father in Oklahoma for a week during that trip.

The Department of Computing Science moved from the oldest building on campus to the University Commons, the newest building. We are now housed in a modern building that connects with a historical building. The blending led to great architectural features and led me to change the way I organize my routine at work. I am spending much more time in the same area with my graduate students. I am truly enjoying the new beginning that this new surrounds allow us.

Scott had an intensive hiking season from July to September. He often joined his friend Stuart Howe from Howe’s the Hike. If you are into hiking, or want to learn about hiking in Canada, you can watch the videos on Stuart’s channel.

I was grounded from traveling for a few months because of a small dental surgery to do a sinus elevation. I took the opportunity to host a Summer games party at the house, to do gardening, take Junior for many long walks, and to enjoy many shows in the Edmonton Theatre Fringe Festival. Scott joined me for some of the Fringe shows when he was in town. In September we had the graduation of my M.Sc. student Quinn Pham, which was attended by my whole research group.

In August we celebrated Daniel’s 21st birthday by going to Olive Garden, his favourite restaurant, and by hosting a few friends in our backyard.

During the Summer and Fall, Scott, Junior, and I sometimes joined Friday’s organized walks through our neighborhood with friends. A favourite thing to do was to find and taste fresh fruit — varieties of raspberries, plums, apples — while walking through back alleys.

The board games and cooking-together parties to gather students, colleagues, and friends at our place were spread throughout the year.

I was fortunate to be able to meet some dear friends during my travels and in Edmonton. Including Daniel Petry and João Campari in São Paulo; John, Tema, Felix, Kathleen, Rita, Dmitri, Olga, my cousin’s daughters Sofia and Carolina with their husbands; and Nova, the newest addition to the family. Running into Fabiola and Marisa by chance at a Christmas concert was very nice.

In October, I traveled back to Brazil, where I co-organized an international workshop in São Paulo to connect Brazilian researchers with industry. It was an intensive couple of days, but it included meeting several colleagues and my first official tour of the city of São Paulo.

Next, I went for a visit to Porto Alegre so that I could take Salete back to a visit to her neurologist and try to support my Mom. On the weekend we went to the very traditional Brique da Redenção and had lunch with Natali.

Bonito, in Mato Grosso do Sul, was the next stop. I attended a conference that is attended by many Brazilian friends and colleagues. It was a great opportunity to see some amazing touristic spots and to visit with colleagues. We went on some amazing activities, such as visiting waterfalls and floating down a crystal clear river full of fish on a sunny day. For our waterfall walk, a local dog took to me and decided to follow me for the entire walk. The local cuisine was also fantastic.

November saw me going back to Toronto for another conference and industry collaboration meetings, allowing me to catch up with my CASCON colleagues.

Now, in December, I traveled back to Brazil to spend a week in Porto Alegre with my Mom and my sister, celebrate Christmas with the family, and to go to our beach house between Christmas and New Year. My brother and his family joined us at the beach house. My long-time friend Horacio — we go back to our undergraduate years in the 1980s — joined us in Porto Alegre and at the beach. It is great to catch up with him over many days.

Meanwhile, Scott and Daniel went to Oklahoma — Daniel agreed to fly this time — to spend time with Scott’s Dad and with others in the family.

All the best in your Winter (or Summer) solstice celebrations.
                Nelson, Scott, Daniel & Junior
